About Theo

read first

He’s a puppy

  • He gets overexcited, has little impulse control and can’t wait for anything. Keep kibble on you to randomly reward him when he’s patient, quiet, or stops at your first “ah-ah” or “drop it”. Make him wait, release him with “okay”, and treat him for waiting.
  • Overexcited, zoomies or biting usually means overtired. Time for a nap.

Chewing & eating

  • He’s at peak teething, so be extra careful about what he can reach. He will ruin it. If a tooth falls out, please save it if you can.
  • He’s still learning what he can and can’t eat. He tries to chew and eat paper towels and wipes, so watch him closely when wiping his paws and face. He has also tried to eat part of a plant, so keep plants out of reach or supervise him closely.
  • Use “leave it” and have a treat ready to reward him when he listens. He’s still working on Leave it and Drop it, and his curiosity is strong, so keep an eye on what’s around him and move things out of the way.
  • Outside the pen, keep him leashed, in the house too (a slip leash is fine). That way you have easy control when you need it and he can’t outrun you. No leash needed for potty trips to the backyard.

Walks & harness

  • No walks needed. He’ll have just had his last vaccine, so the backyard is best for potty and play.
  • Harness: it’s packed, but he’s growing out of it. It still fits but it’s snug and a bit restrictive, so please avoid it unless needed. I’ll get him a new one in Korea.

People & Bori

  • Bori has full authority to correct Theo and put him in his place.
  • He loves people and is easily distracted by new ones.

Handling

  • He hates three things, so pay him in kibble every time: having his harness and leash put on or taken off, being handled or groomed, and having his paws and hygienic areas wiped. Details are in the Training and Grooming tabs.

Food

meals & treats

Meals

  • Daily total: ¾ cup (12 tbsp).
  • No free-feeding and no set meal times. He eats in small tbsp portions spread through the day.
  • Each morning, measure the whole day’s kibble into a container. Then hand it out with a tbsp measuring cup, one scoop at a time, in the snuffle mat, puzzles and training. What’s left in the container is what’s left for the day. If you use a frozen or pre-made treat with kibble in it, like a Kong or lick mat, take that same amount of kibble out of the container first, so he isn’t overfed and the leftover count stays accurate.
  • If possible, play sound desensitization videos during a meal once a day (around 7–15 minutes). Here’s the playlist. We’ve worked up to a moderate volume, but turn it down if he seems bothered.
  • Before his first and last meal of the day, we sing 나 날마다 우리에게. Please sing it with him too.

Treats

  • Frozen Kongs: 1.5 tbsp kibble plus 0.5 tbsp pumpkin puree. Seal the top with 0.5 tbsp Greek yogurt, then freeze.
  • Frozen lick mats: 10 kibbles plus 1.5 tbsp of Greek yogurt and pumpkin puree, then freeze.
  • Freezebones: spread Greek yogurt along the wall, freeze for half an hour, then add kibble and water and freeze again. It can be a meal. I’m bringing three kinds, and the same method works for all of them.

Chews & extras

If he eats ¾ cup of kibble a day, he has about 45 calories to spare for these. Give one, or a mix, until you reach it. Chews need supervision.

  • Charlee Bear treats: cut each one in half. About 1 kcal per half.
  • Puppy rings: I’ve pre-cut them into sticks, each half a ring. Microwave a stick for 5–8 seconds, then give him half a stick (¼ of a ring), in two pieces so it’s easy to grip. About 25 kcal.
  • Enzymatic chew: ½ a chew. About 15 kcal.
  • Turkey tendon (Pupford): about 15–22 kcal per piece.
  • Frozen baby carrot: 1 a day at most. About 4 kcal.

Potty, crate & bedtime

potty & sleep

Potty

  • Supervised potty trip after every nap.
  • How often: outside he can hold it up to 4.5 hours, but at home he goes every 1.5–2 hours.
  • Pad or grass are both fine, but I want him to learn to go outside, so he may not use the pad holder much. Change the pad once a day, not after every pee, and wipe the holder clean with Clorox wipes or similar every few days.
  • He sniffs his poop but won’t eat it.
  • He’s learning the “go potty” cue, so say it as he goes and treat him.
  • Accidents: blot it up, then soak the spot with an enzyme cleaner (Nature’s Miracle or similar) and leave it for 15–20 minutes.

Bedtime routine

  • Bedtime is around 9 PM. He usually wakes between 6:30 and 7:15 AM. Sometimes he wakes early just to potty. If you’re tired, take him out and put him back in the crate. He should go back to sleep for another 30 minutes or so.
  • Water: take the water container off the crate before he goes to sleep. Rinse it, ideally wash it, before you reattach it the next morning. He doesn’t need a separate water bowl.
  • He’s usually good in the crate with the covers on. Without covers he gets anxious.
  • Keep the sound machine on. It helps drown out noise.
  • Naptime ritual: kitchen lights off, crate covers closed, sound machine on, then say “crate”.
  • Overnight he can hold it up to 9 hours, but it’s safer to take him out once in the night. I usually take him out between 11 and 11:30 PM before I go to sleep, or you can do it early in the morning and go back to sleep. The pad holder is fine, so you don’t have to wipe his paws late at night.
  • Waking up: he usually waits patiently until you let him out. If he whines, either it’s been a while and he’s ready to come out, or he needs to potty.

How to ask

training notes
  • “Okay” releases him, ideally with a hand cue. At the crate door he waits on his own until you say it.
  • Entering the pen: he has to be sitting with all four paws on the floor before you step in. If he jumps, say “off” and wait, or turn your back.
  • Leave it and Drop it: trade up for something better and never chase him. Drop it is hardest when he really wants the thing.
  • Quiet: for demand barking, don’t look at or touch him and say nothing. When the barking stops, say “yes!”, then “quiet”, then give the treat. Stretch the silence a little each time.
  • Come: say his name, then “come!” in a happy voice. It’s solid inside the apartment only, so keep him on a leash (a slip leash is fine) anywhere else.

Go carefully

he may bite
  • Harness on/off, grooming and handling are hard for him and he might bite, so proceed with caution, using a slip leash if needed. Treat with kibble the whole time.
  • Harness: it’s snug, so skip it unless needed. If you do use it, treat with kibble when it goes on, again when it comes off, and in between if he stays calm.

Daily grooming

once a day

No baths or nail trims needed. I’ll do those before I drop him off. He can’t be fully groomed until I’m back, so sorry he’ll be so hairy!

Grooming is one of the things he hates. Treat him with kibble throughout, not just at the end. This is when he can bite (hard), so please be careful.

Teeth can be brushed separately, sometime in the day. Treat, brush, then treat again. No toothpaste yet. Use the green silicone finger brush for 30 seconds max. It doesn’t need to be thorough, since he still has puppy teeth. It’s mostly preventive and gets him used to it.

Wipe his paws once a day, since he’ll be playing and going potty in the backyard. Grooming is usually a good time for it. Apply the Dandelion paw balm after. Clean his hygienic areas another time, because you’ll need to hold him: hold him against you with his front paws on your shoulder, like a hug. You can reach everything and he can’t bite you. Treat with kibble the whole time.

  1. Set up. Lay out your tools and set aside a lick mat.
  2. Grooming table. I put his crate mat on my coffee table and lift him up, like a grooming table. A similar surface works.
  3. Detangler. Spray We Love Doodles unscented detangler all over him.
  4. Lick mat, then brush. Put the lick mat down and brush him with the slicker brush while he licks. Do one side, then turn him and the lick mat around and do the other. The worst tangles are around his tail and rear end. Every 5 brushes or so, treat him for being still.
  5. Check for tangles. Go over his body with the long metal comb, using the wide-toothed end, to find any spots that need extra attention.
  6. Face. Use a face wipe to clean the lick mat off his chin and any boogers from his eyes, then brush him out, including his face with the small blue comb.
